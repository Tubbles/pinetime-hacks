# Log — 2026-10-14

## [phone][yacb] 2026-10-14 — a blocked call still rang once: YACB T was in its basic blocking mode

- Report: with YACB T's rules active, a blocked call makes the phone buzz and ring for half a second to a second before it goes quiet, and the watch gets an incoming-call alert. Blocking itself works (notification, blocked-calls log).
- Cause: Yet Another Call Blocker has two blocking modes, and only one of them acts before the ring. "Advanced call blocking mode" (the `useCallScreeningService` switch in `RootSettingsFragment`) binds `CallScreeningServiceImpl` through Android's call-screening role, and the call is rejected inside Telecom's filter stage. Without the role the manifest-registered `PhoneStateBroadcastReceiver` acts instead: it sees the `PHONE_STATE` RINGING broadcast, `PhoneStateHandler.onRinging` evaluates the rules, and `PhoneUtils.endCall` hangs up with `TelecomManager.endCall()`. By then Telecom has added the call, so the ringer has started and every other `PHONE_STATE` receiver has fired. YACB never asks for the role by itself: `PermissionHelper.checkPermissions` at start-up requests runtime permissions only, the role is requested from that Settings switch alone. YACB T is a new package, so the role the original YACB held never carried over, and the runbook's "set it as the caller ID app when it asks" was a step that never happens. Runbook corrected.
- Why advanced mode removes the watch alert too (AOSP main, read 2026-10-14): Telecom's `CallsManager.onCallFilteringComplete` rejects a screened-out call and logs it as `BLOCKED_TYPE` without ever calling `addCall`, and `setCallState` only notifies its listeners for tracked calls (`mCalls.contains(call)`), so `PhoneStateBroadcaster` never reports RINGING. `TelephonyRegistry.notifyCallStateForAllSubs`, which Telecom drives, is the only producer of the `android.intent.action.PHONE_STATE` action. The modem-level `notifyCallState` per subscription does fire during screening, but `broadcastCallStateChanged` turns a valid subscription id into the `android.intent.action.SUBSCRIPTION_PHONE_STATE` action, which neither Gadgetbridge T (`DeviceCommunicationService` registers `PhoneCallReceiver` for `PHONE_STATE` only) nor YACB T's own receiver listens to. So a call rejected by the screening service produces no ring, no watch alert, and no second hang-up attempt.
- How to tell the two modes apart from the phone: a screening-service block lands in Phone T's history as blocked (`Calls.BLOCKED_TYPE`), a basic-mode block as declined, because `TelecomServiceImpl.endCallInternal` turns `endCall()` on a ringing call into `rejectCall`, and `CallLogManager` maps `DisconnectCause.REJECTED` to `REJECTED_TYPE`.
- Residual case with the same symptom: Telecom gives a screening service 5 s (`CallScreeningServiceFilter.CALL_SCREENING_FILTER_TIMEOUT`); a late answer lets the call through and the basic-mode receiver cuts it after the ring starts. YACB T's answer is a contacts query, two local database reads and the in-memory rule stack, so this needs a cold process start on a thrashing phone (the 2026-10-02 memory-pressure state). If it ever shows up on a healthy phone, YACB T's Settings -> Advanced -> Export logcat shows whether `onScreenCall` ran at all for that call. Gadgetbridge T's "Call notification delay" (seconds, notifications settings) is the blunt mitigation for the watch half: the delayed send is cancelled when the call ends inside the delay.
